# Specials costing

Open **Specials costing** in Culinary & Events, or **Cost a special** on Recipes. Managers/admins can build a private one-off costing without creating shared recipe records. Set the dish yield and menu price per yield unit; add catalog ingredients, existing recipes, or temporary sub-recipes. Add temporary sub-recipes to the dish (or another sub-recipe) by the amount used. Nested costs use the same unit conversion and reviewed edible-yield calculations as regular recipes.

**Save costing** stores the full working structure in the signed-in user's account. Saved costings appear at the top. Save before leaving; an unsaved-change warning protects page exits. Another tab's newer save produces a conflict instead of overwriting it. Prices recalculate from the current catalog when reopened, so these are working costings, not frozen historical price quotes. Temporary recipes stay inside the saved costing. Missing prices, incompatible units, missing sources and circular recipe links are flagged; printing is disabled until resolved.

**Print costing & ingredients** includes the dish and all reachable nested recipe cost sheets. Ingredient rows disclose as-purchased price (AP), reviewed edible yield, edible-portion cost (EP) where enabled, and purchased quantity. Counts stay whole purchased items; trim is applied only to eligible weight/volume quantities. Unreviewed ingredients retain legacy costing and explicitly say their yield has not been reviewed.

Accounts already have a **Home venue** under Users, independently of access role. This now supplies the initial venue for a new special and an invoice with no existing venue. Saved invoice details and recognized receiving venues take precedence; users can change venue before saving. Access roles alone do not identify which kitchen someone works in.

Ingredient sparklines require two comparable approved positive purchases from the same supplier. The empty state now explains this requirement. Full invoice price history is available on each ingredient page; credits, voids, non-food purchases and incompatible price units do not establish a comparable trend.
